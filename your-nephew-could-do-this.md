# Your nephew could do this

A friend's wife sells ethnic wear on Instagram. Tens of thousands of followers. Orders via DMs and WhatsApp. No website, no proper store.

He asked if I could help get something set up.

I spent a few hours on it. Not in the way you might expect.

---

I did not open Shopify and click through menus. I did not manually upload images, configure sections, or create pages by hand.

I described what I wanted. Claude Code — with Playwright running alongside it — built the store. Slideshow with three slides. A four-column footer. An About page. A Contact page. Four policy pages. Navigation menus linking everything together. An announcement bar. When it was done, I opened the live store on my phone.

Everything worked. I had barely touched the keyboard.

---

This is not magic. It is browser automation in service of a well-specified goal. Claude Code used Playwright — a tool that controls a browser programmatically — to perform the same actions a human would. Click here. Fill this. Select that. Save. Navigate to the next screen. Repeat.

The difference is that I did not need to know *how* to do any of this. I needed to know *what* I wanted. That distinction is the one worth sitting with.

Knowing what you want and knowing how to execute it used to be bundled together. You had to understand the tool to produce the outcome. Now they are separable. The what is yours. The how belongs to the agent.

---

After the store was live, I sent a message to my nephew.

He's in his twenties. Not a developer. No particular technical background.

I told him what I'd built and how I'd built it. I told him the store needed one more round — the actual products loaded, the real photos swapped in, payments configured. And I told him he could take it from here. That I'd be there to guide him through every step.

More than that: I told him this is worth learning. Not because of this one project, but because every business in the next few years will need someone who can do this. Someone who can sit down with an operator, understand what they need, and use AI tools to build it.

What used to require a developer, a designer, a project manager, and weeks of back-and-forth now takes a few hours and the ability to describe what you want.

---

This is not a story about replacing developers. Complex systems still need skilled engineers. That is not changing.

But the straightforward work — the kind small business owners pay agencies thousands for, the kind that drags on because of coordination overhead and handoffs and revision rounds — that work is now available to anyone willing to learn the tools and who has seen it done once.

My nephew has not done this before. But he has a mentor who has. That, increasingly, is enough.

The agency work is not disappearing. It is looking for new owners.

Your nephew could do this. So could you.
