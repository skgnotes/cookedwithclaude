### Closing the books

The task: process a full year of consulting income from a bank statement.

The EA had compiled the statement. What it needed was analysis: total the income line by line, verify it against expected figures, calculate the tax withheld (recorded separately from the income itself), add up receivables that hadn't yet landed, and build a clean cash position snapshot.

The output: income for the year verified. Tax withheld calculated. Cash in bank plus receivables equals total position. One number on a page, supported by the full breakdown behind it.

That number matters not because it's impressive but because it's accurate. There's a specific kind of mental overhead that comes from carrying an approximate figure — you know the rough shape of things but not the exact position. Replacing that with a verified number removes the overhead. You know where you stand.

One item was flagged for follow-up: the formal tax record needed to be checked before filing, to confirm the withheld amount matched what was actually recorded with the government. That's a separate step, done through a separate portal. Noted and staged.

### The log

On the same day, a smaller piece of housekeeping: the session log format was fixed.

The log had been collecting two kinds of entries: session starts (Claude Code opening, noting what it was about to do) and session wraps (what actually got done). The start entries were noise — they repeated information that the wrap captured better, without adding anything. They were removed. The log now captures outcomes only.

This is the kind of change you make once and never think about again. The log is cleaner. Future sessions start with a more useful history. The cost of making the change was fifteen minutes.

### The day that was.

A year's income verified. A cash position known. A log format fixed. One follow-up item staged.

What strikes me about the beginning of this record is how ordinary it is. No automation breakthrough, no product launched, no dramatic before-and-after. Just: the books are closed, the numbers are right, the system is a little more honest about what it actually captures.

That's where every good system starts. Not with the impressive parts. With the parts that make the impressive parts possible.

You can build this. Not all at once. Not in a day. But piece by piece, the way I've been doing it — one automation, one workflow, one small win at a time. And if you want help getting started, that's exactly what this site is for.
