# My Day with Claude Code — March 21, 2026

Some days feel like you've lived three of them back to back. Yesterday was that day.

<div style="background: #f0f0f7; border-radius: 6px; padding: 16px 20px; margin-bottom: 24px;" markdown="1">

**Quick read**

Somewhere in the middle of this day, a publishing team of AI agents went live and ran four articles while I was at lunch with my parents. The day itself started early and ended near midnight. Here's what happened:

- Connected my note-taking app to Claude — one thought captured, lands in two places automatically
- Ordered cigarettes from Swiggy with a single command (it half-broke; fixed it afterward — that's how this works)
- Gave AI agents their own email addresses and the ability to read verification codes off my phone — then used that to buy a water bottle from Amazon without touching the keyboard once
- Built a full AI publishing team: strategist, writer, publisher, quality checker — all handing off to each other automatically, no human in the loop
- Four articles went live on this site while I was at lunch across town
- Ran a month's worth of usage data: 1,056 sessions, 239 hours, 89% satisfaction

The whole thing ran on one AI assistant, a decent amount of coffee, and — for part of it — just a phone.

</div>

<p style="margin-bottom: 32px;"></p>

### Housekeeping

It started with housekeeping — the kind that doesn't feel glamorous but you know matters. My content system for this site had gotten messy. Ideas, drafts, published articles, random captures — all in the same place, all mixed together. So Claude and I sorted it out: a dedicated backlog, a separate inbox for fresh ideas, everything in its own place. Like reorganising a desk that had gotten out of hand. Then we made sure everything that talks to each other was still talking correctly — a few configurations that had drifted were brought back in line. None of this is exciting. All of it needed to happen.

While we were at it, I updated a page on the live site, pushed the change, and Claude verified it was actually live — not a cached version — before moving on. That level of verification used to require me to check manually. Now it just happens.

Then something I'd been meaning to set up: I connected my note-taking app (Notion) directly to Claude. Now when I capture a thought — an idea, a task, a note — it lands in two places simultaneously without me doing anything extra. One action, two destinations. Nothing falls through the cracks.

### One command

Somewhere in the middle of all this, I ran a command to order cigarettes from Swiggy. One command. The order is placed, payment goes through, delivery is dispatched. It hit a snag midway — had to step in once manually — so afterward we found the bug and fixed it. That's how this works: you build it, it runs, when something breaks you fix it and it runs better next time. The goal is automation that you trust, and you build that trust over iterations.

### Giving agents an address

Then we did something that sounds abstract until you understand the implication: we gave the AI agents their own presence in the world.

If you're going to have AI working on your behalf — placing orders, sending emails, completing transactions — those agents need to be able to receive and act on information, just like a real employee would. So we set them up with their own email addresses, and connected them to my phone in such a way that they can read incoming verification codes directly. No more copy-pasting a one-time password from my phone to a form. The agent reads it, enters it, and the transaction completes.

Which brings us to the water bottle.

I ordered a Boldfit 2.2L water bottle from Amazon. Claude found the product, added it to the cart, selected the payment method, waited for the verification code to arrive on my phone, read it, entered it, and completed the purchase. I didn't touch the keyboard once after the initial request. ₹898, arriving Tuesday. That's not a demo. That's how I shop now.

### The publishing team

The main event of the afternoon was the agent team.

I've been building a small publishing operation for this site — not just writing articles, but running the whole pipeline: deciding what to write, drafting it, publishing it, checking it for quality before it goes live. That entire workflow is now handled by a team of AI agents, each with a specific role. A strategist that decides what article to write next. A writer that produces the draft. A publisher that puts it live on the site. A quality checker that reviews it first. They hand off to each other automatically. When one finishes, the next picks up. I get a notification on Telegram at each step.

It took some work to get the team running cleanly — the early runs had some misfires, agents getting ahead of themselves. Like onboarding any new team, you set the protocols, you test, you fix what breaks, you run it again. By the third run it was clean.

### Lunch

Then I went to lunch with my parents.

And while I was sitting at the table — talking, eating, just being present — my phone kept buzzing. An article topic had been selected. A draft was written. It was reviewed and published. Then another one started. By the time I got back, four articles had gone live on this website while I was having lunch across town.

That's the most surreal thing that happened yesterday. Not any single feature or automation. Just sitting across from my parents and getting notified, quietly, that things were getting done.

### After lunch

After lunch, we kept going. I refined how the team selects topics — a balance between planned content and whatever's freshest in the idea inbox. Added the quality-checking step. Set up a professional LinkedIn presence for one of the agents, so it can interact professionally in the world. Then did a cleanup pass on the site itself — 47 articles that had slowly accumulated some inconsistencies in formatting. All fixed.

Late in the day, I ran a usage report across all my Claude Code sessions for the past month. **1,056 sessions. 239 hours. 89% satisfaction.** The final pipeline run of the day — another article — was kicked off before I closed the laptop. Telegram would tell me when it was done.

### The day that was.

Content organised. Tools connected. Errands automated. A publishing team built and run. A water bottle ordered hands-free. A website cleaned up. Lunch with my parents.

What strikes me, sitting with it now, is how much of this was just… done. Not planned across a week. Not delegated to a team. Done in a single day, with one AI assistant and a decent amount of coffee — and some of it from my phone, while the agents pinged me on Telegram from the other room. There's something quietly extraordinary about that — and also something that makes me wonder what it'll feel like in six months when days like this are just normal.

I think they already almost are.

You can build this. Not all at once. Not in a day. But piece by piece, the way I've been doing it — one automation, one workflow, one small win at a time. And if you want help getting started, that's exactly what this site is for.
